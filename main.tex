\subsection{Foundations of Fractal Image Compression}
The theoretical framework of Fractal Image Compression (FIC) originates from the application of the fractal transform via Iterated Function Systems (IFS) \cite{Barnsley1985}. This mathematical foundation utilizes the Collage Theorem and the Contractive Mapping Fixed-Point Theorem to systematically exploit block self-affine redundancies inherent within digital images \cite{barnsley2014fractals, Barnsley1985}. Early implementations demonstrated the feasibility of compressing images through these multi-scale fractal mapping principles \cite{barnsley1988better}, which ultimately led to the development of automated, block-based practical compression schemes \cite{FICOriginal}.

In the standardized block-oriented framework, encoding a gray-level image of size $N \times N$ requires partitioning the spatial domain into a range pool $R$ comprised of $(N/L)^2$ non-overlapping range blocks $v$ of dimensions $L \times L$ \cite{FICOriginal}. Concurrently, a domain pool $D$ is established by extracting $(N - 2L + 1)^2$ overlapping domain blocks of size $2L \times 2L$ \cite{FICOriginal}. For each distinct range block $v \in R$, a search is executed across the virtual codebook of the domain pool to map a contractive affine transformation $\Phi$ from the domain to the range block space \cite{FICOriginal}.

To evaluate similarity metrics, each candidate domain block $u$ is down-sampled to match the $L \times L$ resolution of the target range block. Spatial orientation constraints are handled by subjecting the down-sampled domain block to eight canonical Dihedral transformations (isometries) $T_k$ ($k = 0, 1, \dots, 7$), producing an array of transformed domain blocks $u_k$, where $u_0 = u$ \cite{FICOriginal}. These geometric operations include horizontal reflection ($T_1$), vertical reflection ($T_2$), combined horizontal-vertical reflection ($T_3$), and diagonal reflections ($T_4$ through $T_7$).

The discrete block-wise contractive affine transformation maps spatial coordinates and intensity metrics simultaneously:
\begin{equation}
\Phi \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = p \begin{bmatrix} a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} t_x \\ t_y \end{bmatrix} + q
\end{equation}
where the $2 \times 2$ sub-matrix represents the Dihedral transformation operator $T_k$, $(t_x, t_y)$ establishes the spatial coordinates of the matching domain block, $p$ defines the contrast scaling factor ($\alpha$), and $q$ denotes the brightness offset ($\Delta g$) \cite{FICOriginal}.

Parameter optimization is modeled as an inverse problem minimized using a Mean Squared Error (MSE) or Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) distortion metric \cite{FICOriginal}. For each pairing, the specific isometry index $d$ is determined via:
\begin{equation}
d = \arg\min_{k \in \{0,1,\dots,7\}} \left\{ \text{MSE}(v, p_k u_k + q_k) \right\}
\end{equation}
where the discrete MSE over an $L \times L$ pixel grid is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\text{MSE}(v, u) = \frac{1}{L^2} \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} \left( v(i,j) - u(i,j) \right)^2
\end{equation}
Applying least-squares regression to minimize this distortion objective yields closed-form solutions for the massic parameters $p$ and $q$:
\begin{equation}
p = \frac{L^2 \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} v(i,j) u_k(i,j) - \left( \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} v(i,j) \right) \left( \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} u_k(i,j) \right)}{L^2 \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} u_k(i,j)^2 - \left( \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} u_k(i,j) \right)^2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
q = \frac{1}{L^2} \left[ \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} v(i,j) - p \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} \sum_{j=0}^{L-1} u_k(i,j) \right]
\end{equation}

The resulting code vectors—comprising spatial coordinates $(t_x, t_y)$, orientation index $d$, scale factor $p$, and offset $q$—are uniformly quantized and bit-allocated for transmission. Image reconstruction at the decoder operates recursively by applying these mapped transformations onto an arbitrary initial image profile. Based on the Partitioned Iteration Function System (PIFS) model and Banach's fixed-point theorem, the processing sequence yields a convergent Cauchy sequence that terminates at a unique invariant attractor image.

\subsection{Acceleration Techniques in FIC}
Although capable of guaranteeing high compression ratios and reconstruction fidelity, conventional FIC is constrained by an exhaustive search strategy \cite{241507}. Because each independent range block must iterate through every permutation of domain blocks across all eight orientations, the computational overhead is exceedingly high, limiting real-time practical utility \cite{241507}.

To bypass this computational bottleneck, subsequent literature has introduced alternative structural and algorithmic paradigms. Range block search spaces have been constrained through quad-tree partitioning topologies \cite{compression1995fractal} and hierarchical tree-structured block classification methods \cite{478245, hurtgen1993fast} to group blocks by geometric or statistical features. Concurrently, parallel processing arrays and custom hardware engine architectures have been leveraged to distribute matching operations across distinct SIMD hardware elements \cite{hufnagl2000algorithms, vidya2000architecture}.

Metaheuristic and stochastic optimization algorithms provide another avenue to replace exhaustive search mechanics. Evolutionary schemes, including schema-based or spatial-correlation Genetic Algorithms (GAs), map block coordinates directly to chromosomes to quickly navigate search matrices by utilizing structural spatial regularities \cite{wu2007schema, wu2006spatial}.

\subsection{Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)}
Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is a population-based stochastic optimization methodology modeled after the group intelligence and social flight dynamics observed in biological swarms \cite{PSOOriginal, eberhart1995new}. The underlying algorithm structures a swarm of candidate solutions, termed particles, to evaluate a multi-dimensional objective search space. For a problem domain of dimensionality $D$, each particle $i$ tracks a time-varying position vector $X_i = (x_{i1}, x_{i2}, \dots, x_{iD})$ and a corresponding trajectory velocity vector $V_i = (v_{i1}, v_{i2}, \dots, v_{iD})$.

Throughout execution, individual particles update their state vectors using memory profiles of their personal historical best-performing coordinates, denoted as $\text{P}_{\text{BEST}, i}$. Simultaneously, global communication routes enable the swarm to maintain a reference to the global best position achieved by any population element, defined as $\text{G}_{\text{BEST}}$. At each discrete evolutionary generation step $t$, the velocity and position updates are implemented using the following formulations:
\begin{equation}
V_i(t+1) = w \cdot V_i(t) + c_1 \cdot r_1 \cdot (\text{P}_{\text{BEST}, i} - X_i(t)) + c_2 \cdot r_2 \cdot (\text{G}_{\text{BEST}} - X_i(t))
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
X_i(t+1) = X_i(t) + V_i(t+1)
\end{equation}
where $w$ is the inertia weight regulating global exploration versus local exploitation boundaries; $c_1$ and $c_2$ denote the cognitive and social acceleration coefficients; and $r_1, r_2 \sim U(0,1)$ represent uniform stochastic variables that inject randomized perturbations to prevent premature stagnation inside local optima \cite{PSOOriginal}.


\subsection{Metaheuristic Optimization Frameworks in Fractal Image Compression}
To mitigate the exponential computational complexity inherent in exhaustive combinatorial block-matching search spaces, population-based and swarm-driven stochastic metaheuristics are widely integrated into the Fractal Image Compression (FIC) pipeline \cite{241507, 478245}. By encoding the structural parameters of the fractal mapping into mathematical optimization arrays, the conventional exhaustive scan across the domain pool is re-engineered into an intelligence-driven localization problem over a constrained fitness landscape \cite{FICOriginal}.

\subsubsection{Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) Integration}
Integrating swarm intelligence with fractal processes yields a highly efficient alternative to traditional block encoding acceleration frameworks \cite{PSOFIC}. In contrast to standard evolutionary algorithms that rely purely on competitive selection, swarm-based candidate structures navigate solution topologies by balancing localized personal discovery ($\text{P}_{\text{BEST}}$) with global collective information propagation ($\text{G}_{\text{BEST}}$) \cite{PSOOriginal, eberhart1995new}. 

When mapped onto the discrete FIC domain, each particle within the swarm represents a candidate contractive transformation vector \cite{PSOFIC}. Specifically, the multi-dimensional spatial coordinates of a particle define the discrete spatial offset location $(t_x, t_y)$ of a domain block within the virtual codebook pool, while secondary vector elements concurrently manage the discrete orientation mapping index $d \in \{0, 1, \dots, 7\}$ and the continuous massic parameters $p$ and $q$ \cite{FICOriginal, PSOFIC}. This intelligence-driven localized trajectory tracking removes the computational requirement for exhaustive domain pool scanning. By executing directed velocity updates over a constrained fitness landscape evaluated directly against a Mean Squared Error (MSE) objective function, this methodology achieves order-of-magnitude acceleration in encoding velocities while strictly preserving peak signal-to-noise ratios (PSNR) for high-precision imaging applications such as medical imaging \cite{PSOFIC}.

\subsubsection{Genetic Algorithms (GAs)}
Alongside swarm-based architectures, Genetic Algorithms (GAs) operate as global search heuristics modeled upon the principles of natural selection and genomic inheritance to navigate the discrete codebook matrices \cite{mitra1998technique, wu2007schema, wu2006spatial}. A canonical GA maintains a population of candidate spatial transformation vectors, abstractly encoded as chromosomes, which undergo iterative refinement across successive generations via three fundamental operators:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{Selection:} A probabilistic mechanism that prioritizes individuals exhibiting superior fitness metrics (minimal MSE), ensuring that high-affinity block-matching characteristics are preserved in the mating pool \cite{mitra1998technique, wu2006spatial}.
    \item \textit{Crossover:} The primary exploratory operator, which executes systematic recombination of genetic segments between paired parental chromosomes to traverse uncharted regions of the domain search space \cite{wu2007schema}.
    \item \textit{Mutation:} A low-probability stochastic perturbation applied to individual alleles, injecting genotypic diversity to prevent premature convergence and stagnation within sub-optimal local basins.
\end{enumerate}

While GAs demonstrate exceptional robustness in macro-spatial exploration and the identification of promising global regions of the domain pool, their randomized operational mechanics are fundamentally blind to localized topological gradients. Consequently, standard GAs exhibit poor fine-tuning capabilities and slow asymptotic convergence in the vicinity of localized extrema, occasionally generating localized pixel artifacts during the fractal decoding stage \cite{wu2006spatial}.

\subsubsection{Memetic Algorithms (MAs) and Local Search Integration}
To resolve the exploration-exploitation imbalance inherent in pure evolutionary search, the paradigm of Memetic Algorithms (MAs) is established, introducing a hybrid metaheuristic architecture inspired by cultural transmission and individual learning dynamics. MAs systematically embed deterministic or heuristic Local Search (LS) strategies—such as Hill Climbing, Simulated Annealing, or Tabu Search—directly within the global evolutionary loop of the GA to fine-tune the spatial coordinates of the domain mapping. 

Mathematically, let $\mathcal{X}$ denote the discrete optimization domain of codebook coordinates, and let $f: \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ represent the MSE objective function to be minimized \cite{FICOriginal}. While the evolutionary operators execute coarse-grained global routing across the domain pool, the memetic component applies a localized optimization mapping $\Lambda: \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathcal{X}$ to an individual $x \in \mathcal{X}$ within its immediate neighborhood structure $\mathcal{N}(x)$, enforcing strict monotonic convergence:
\begin{equation}
f(\Lambda(x)) \le f(x)
\end{equation}
By offloading the fine-grained tracking of the exact coordinate offsets to the exploitative local search operator, the algorithm avoids premature convergence while maximizing reconstruction fidelity.

\subsection{Synthesis of Metaheuristic Accelerators in FIC}
The architectural mapping of population-based and swarm-driven metaheuristics onto the FIC framework fundamentally redefines the classical codebook search. By treating the domain block properties as low-dimensional state variables within an optimization vector, the search space is converted from an exhaustive spatial scan into an intelligence-driven localization problem \cite{FICOriginal, PSOFIC, mitra1998technique, wu2006spatial}. 

Whether utilizing the socio-stochastic velocity trajectories of PSO—which stands as one of the most prominent paradigms due to its balance of global communication and memory retention \cite{PSOOriginal, PSOFIC}—the global exploratory mechanisms of GAs \cite{mitra1998technique, wu2006spatial}, or the high-fidelity exploitative refinement of hybrid MAs, these metaheuristics focus computational bandwidth exclusively on high-affinity regions of the domain pool. This integration bypasses the exhaustive evaluation loops that constrain conventional FIC frameworks \cite{FICOriginal, 241507}. Consequently, the combination of evolutionary frameworks with local search strategies yields order-of-magnitude acceleration while strictly preserving the mathematical convergence guarantees and reconstruction fidelity dictated by the contractive mapping fixed-point theorem \cite{Barnsley1985, barnsley2014fractals}.
